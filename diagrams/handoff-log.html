Atmospherics Room — Handoff

Base life support + waste CO2 scrubbing, shared between the human base and a connected plant base. Designed and scripted, not yet run in-game. Written so a fresh chat can pick this up cold, focused on this build only.

Room & Target

9 × 15 × 2

Target: a single shared atmosphere, 60% N2 / 20% O2 / 20% CO2 at 100 kPa, feeding both the human base and a connected plant base (12 plants currently, expanding — each draws ≈8.6 mol/hr CO2 and returns ≈8.6 mol/hr O2). CO2 is still actively targeted by Regulation, just raised from 5% to 20% (2026-08-02) — N2 absorbed the difference (75% → 60%), O2 unchanged at 20%. One shared mix, not split — Ryan has directly confirmed CO2 up to 20% is fine for humans in this game, overriding the wiki's >1%-causes-impairment claim, which is exactly why the target was raised to that level rather than left at 5%.

Large-Scale Collection Redesign

2026-08-02 · replaces old Mars Intake + Cooling zones

Second base build: a larger, standalone Mars-air collection & CO2-liquefaction pipeline sized around Large Powered Vents and a Large Tank storage bank, scripted from scratch. This replaces the old Intake/Compression script's approach — not just scaled up, but corrected in two ways the research below turned up.

What changed vs. the old design:

  • Corrected 2026-08-02 Vents left always-On was wrong — Powered Vents have no built-in pressure limiter. First draft of this redesign assumed vents were self-limiting and could run unscripted forever. That's true of the Active Vent (it has PressureInternal/PressureExternal caps that stop it from over-pressurizing its own side), but the Powered Vent family explicitly does not have that internal limiter — confirmed via community sources. Left on continuously against a closed pipe network, it will keep pumping Mars atmosphere in past the pipe's burst point. This is what was blowing pipes. Fixed below: vents are now IC-gated on raw-buffer pressure, same hysteresis pattern as the pump bank.
  • Corrected 2026-08-02 "Compressor" is not an actual in-game item. Every version of this design, including the original pre-session artifact, called for a "Compressor" device — it doesn't exist in Stationeers. The real device for forcibly moving gas between two pipe networks (including against a large pressure differential, which is exactly what the raw→HP stage needs) is the Turbo Volume Pump. Renamed throughout: script aliases are now pump1/pump2, not compressor1/compressor2. The IC10 logic itself doesn't change — Turbo Volume Pump uses the same On logic type as everything else here — only the device you actually place and wire changes.
  • Fixed Still no guessed HASH() strings anywhere in the control path. The old script batch-addressed vents via a guessed HASH("StructurePoweredVentLarge"). The new design avoids that too — vents are direct-wired to IC pins instead, same as the Turbo Volume Pumps. If you have more vents or pumps than one housing's 6 pins allow, duplicate the housing (reading the same shared pressure sensor) rather than reaching for batch-by-hash — avoid unverifiable hashes in a script you're trusting to not blow up your base.
  • Bug found The old CO2-drain logic almost certainly never fired. atmospherics_intake_compression.ic10 used define CO2_COND_TEMP 0 assuming Celsius, but the Temperature logic type in Stationeers IC10 reports Kelvin, not Celsius. slt r6 r1 CO2_COND_TEMP was therefore checking "is the line colder than absolute zero" — never true. valve3 (the CO2 drain) would have stayed shut regardless of actual pipe temperature. Old script left in place below for reference/history, but treat it as non-functional until that line is fixed.
  • Rev B, 2026-08-04 Companion P&ID simplified and corrected. Rev A drew a separate "IC Housing" chip with leader lines to every device — one of those chips ended up rendered on top of a pump in Area 600, an overlap caught by direct review. Rev B removes the chips entirely (pin number now lives inline on each device's own tag, e.g. PA-201 (d0)) and relabels every sensor from generic ISA codes (PT/TT/AT) to the real in-game devices: Pipe Analyzer (tag PA, on a pipe network) or Gas Sensor (tag GS, in open atmosphere). Area numbers (100–800) used there now also appear next to each zone and script here for cross-reference.
  • 2026-08-04 Recapture now also scrubs Pollutant and Volatiles, not just CO2. Two more Filtration units added to Area 800 (filtrationPollutant, filtrationVolatiles), each independently gated on bare presence (RatioPollutant/RatioVolatiles > 0) rather than a hysteresis band — unlike CO2, there's no acceptable target level for either, so any trace at all triggers that filter. recyclePump now runs whenever any of the three filters is active (OR via max), not just on the CO2 trigger.
  • 2026-08-04 CO2 Supply now reuses the collection pipeline's own liquid CO2 instead of re-extracting from raw Mars air a second time. Ryan pointed out the redundancy directly: Area 300 already condenses CO2 to liquid, and Area 400 was separately filtering fresh Mars air for CO2 — two subsystems doing overlapping work. The liquid CO2 was previously just parked unused in the Liquid Tank. Fixed by draining it with a Purge Valve (liquidPurge, Setting fixed at 0) instead of a Digital Valve + Filtration — the valve continuously skims off whatever CO2 evaporates from the liquid, which also encourages more to evaporate, net-converting liquid back to gas. A backup Condensation Valve sits on the gas output to catch anything that re-condenses before reaching the buffer tank, per documented community practice. Area 400 dropped from 3 pins to 2 (no more Filtration unit needed — the liquid was already pure CO2, nothing left to filter out).
  • Simplified Liquid CO2 separation is now a passive device, not scripted logic. The Condensation Valve does one-way, automatic drainage of any liquid phase out of a gas pipe into a liquid pipe network — no On/Off, no IC10 interface at all (its control mode is manual/passive by design, confirmed via community sources). Plumb it directly into the cooled HP line with its liquid side running to a Liquid Tank, and it separates CO2 the moment it condenses. This is strictly more reliable than a temperature-gated Digital Valve guessing at a threshold — it reacts to the actual physical phase, not a sensor reading of it.
  • 2026-08-07 N2 and O2 no longer share one tank — Area 300 split into two IC-gated outputs, and the raw/liquid buffers went insulated. Ryan asked for both directly: separate storage for the Mars-collected N2 and O2 instead of one combined bank, and Large Tank (Insulated) / Large Liquid Tank (Insulated) for the raw Mars-air buffer (TK-101, ×1) and the liquid CO2 tanks (TK-301, ×2) respectively — both real Stationeers structures, built from insulated kits, confirmed via web search. Area 300 is no longer passive: atmospherics_gassplit.ic10 chains an N2 filter then an O2 filter off the post-condensation gas line, each into its own hysteresis-gated output (N2 → its own tank, O2 → Area 500's O2 tank bank). The previous single "N2/O2 ≈66/33" tank bank fed both of Regulation's n2Pump and o2Pump off the same mixed pipe, which doesn't actually work — a pump can't selectively pull one gas out of a mix.
  • 2026-08-07 Recapture (Area 800) now sorts N2 too, and nothing it captures goes back to the room directly. Ryan's direction: any gas Recapture pulls out of the room air should be filtered and returned to its own storage tank, and the only path back into the room atmosphere should be Regulation's three pumps. Added a fifth filter (N2, → Area 300's TK-302) and removed the old "scrubbed remainder → back to Area 700" bus entirely — O2/CO2/N2 each route to their tank, Pollutant/Volatiles vent to exterior, and there's nothing left over to return. This pushed the loop to 7 controlled devices (1 sensor + 1 pump + 5 filters), over one IC housing's 6-pin limit, so it's now split across atmospherics_recapture.ic10 (the real decision logic: sensor, pump, CO2/Pollutant/Volatiles) and a new companion atmospherics_recapture_riders.ic10 (3 pins, just mirrors the pump's On state onto the O2 and N2 filters — same "duplicate the housing" pattern used elsewhere in this build rather than reaching for HASH()-based batch addressing).
  • 2026-08-07 Regulation's three pumps upgraded to Turbo Volume Pump. n2Pump/o2Pump/co2Pump were plain Volume Pumps; switched to Turbo Volume Pump for faster response to the room's demand swings, same pattern as Area 200's compression bank. No logic change — Turbo Volume Pump uses the same On logic type, only the device placed and wired changes.
  • 2026-08-07 Electrolyzer replaced with an Ice Crusher — crushed Oxite and Nitrice instead of ice-fed electrolysis. Ryan's call, after asking whether it made more sense than the Electrolyzer: an Ice Crusher fed Oxite outputs roughly 90% O2 / 10% N2 directly (real device fact, confirmed via wiki), which conveniently matches the same split Area 300 already does, and it skips the Electrolyzer's H2 byproduct and autoignition risk entirely — no safety-trip logic needed for this housing. atmospherics_icecrusher.ic10 replaces atmospherics_electrolyzer.ic10; its O2 and N2 outputs feed the same shared tanks atmospherics_gassplit.ic10 already established (TK-302 for N2, the shared O2 network for O2) rather than standing up a second tank pair.
  • 2026-08-07 Nitrice adds a third gas to this build — N2O — and it needs very different handling than everything else piped here. Ryan is also running Nitrice through a second Ice Crusher for N2O. N2O condenses far more easily than O2/N2/CO2: its in-game triple point sits around −21°C (confirmed directly from the Stationpedia phase graph, not just community sources), so the branch has to stay at LOW pressure and room temperature on purpose, opposite of the CO2 line's deliberate high-pressure/cold-radiator approach. New housing atmospherics_n2osupply.ic10 (4 pins) keeps its buffer target at just 1,000/800 kPa — far below every other supply buffer in this build — and keeps the whole run indoors, away from any exterior/cold pipe segment.
  • 2026-08-07 Relief valves added to every tank in the build, not just the two that already had them. Ryan's direction directly: TK-101 (Area 100) and the HP line (Area 200) already had relief backstops; TK-302/the O2 network (Area 300), TK-301/TK-401 (Area 400), and the new N2O buffer (Area 500) didn't. All now vent to exterior on the same hysteresis pattern, trip well above their operating target with margin under the 60,000 kPa tank rating (except TK-301, which uses the much lower 6,080 kPa liquid pipe burst rating instead). TK-301's relief uses a Liquid Digital Valve, not the regular gas-side Digital Valve used everywhere else — confirmed as a real, separate device by Ryan directly after this was flagged as unverified.
  • 2026-08-07 Emergency buffer tank added to Area 800's room-air intake. Ryan added a passive tank on the "blue line" (the shared intake feeding both Recapture and the Ice Crusher branch) as a reserve of room air in case the base depressurizes for any reason. Purely passive, no IC10 pins — just physical volume sitting on the intake line.
  • 2026-08-07 Pollutant and Methane/Hydrogen now captured to storage tanks instead of vented — a Volatile Ice safety contingency. Ryan's actual tank layout has dedicated storage for CO2, O2, N2, Pollutants, N2O, and a combined mix tank for Methane + Hydrogen — the last one exists because Volatile Ice melts into Methane + Hydrogen if it ends up inside the base (hauled in, dropped, whatever happens), and neither gas belongs in the room atmosphere. Rule: any Pollutant, Methane, or Hydrogen reading at all should trigger scrubbing until it clears, same bare-presence pattern already used for Pollutant, now covering Methane and Hydrogen too — each filter only turns on for its own gas(es), not just because the pump happens to be running. Confirmed along the way: "Volatiles" was renamed to "Methane" in a game update (same gas, same RatioVolatiles logic now reads as RatioMethane), and Hydrogen is a genuinely separate gas type that had no handling anywhere in this build until now — the old Electrolyzer's H2 byproduct was just vented, and that housing is gone. filtrationMethaneH2 is one Filtration unit with both cartridges loaded (multiple cartridges in one unit mix their catch together, which is exactly what's wanted here since both go to the same mix tank).
  • 2026-08-08 Every alias in every script renamed to be globally unique, and every "Pipe Analyzer / Gas Sensor" ambiguous device comment resolved to one real device. Ryan's direction: all 10 IC housings sit on the same data network, so alias names only being unique within their own script isn't good enough — a name collision between two housings on the same network is a real risk once they're actually wired up together. Every alias across all 9 active scripts now carries an area-number suffix (_100, _200, _300, _400, _500, _500b, _600, _800, _800r) — verified duplicate-free across all 44 aliases. Also fixed: three device comments that said "Pipe Analyzer / Gas Sensor" as if a pin could be either — a pin is wired to one specific device, never both. Ryan caught this on rawSensor in atmospherics_intake.ic10 first; the same ambiguity turned up on tankSensor (atmospherics_compression.ic10) and co2Sensor (atmospherics_co2supply.ic10) too. All three resolve to Pipe Analyzer — each one reads a pipe network's Pressure, not open-room atmosphere, which only a Gas Sensor does. The superseded atmospherics_intake_compression.ic10 block below is left un-renamed on purpose — it's historical reference, not part of the live network.

Researched Facts

web-verified, 2026-08-02 — used to set the constants above
  • CO2 triple point: 218 K / 517 kPa. Below 517 kPa, CO2 can only be solid or gas — never liquid, regardless of temperature. Our 20,000 kPa target has huge margin above this.Stationeers Community Wiki, corroborated via search
  • At low pressure (roughly the old 3500 kPa target), CO2 starts condensing below about 265 K (−8.1°C).Stationeers Community Wiki / player reports
  • At ≈19,000 kPa, players report CO2 beginning to condense around 296 K (23°C) — i.e. near room temperature, no aggressive refrigeration needed once you're compressing that hard.Steam Community discussion, player-reported — not wiki-table-verified, treat as directional
  • Large Powered Vent moves 4× the flow of a standard Active Vent (~5,400 L needed to keep up with max outward flow at 100 kPa external vs. ~900 L for a standard Active Vent); scavenges structures across a 6-large-grid Manhattan radius, built specifically for large-scale rooms/hangars.Steam Community discussion
  • Large Tank: 50,000 L capacity, 60,000 kPa burst pressure.Stationeers Community Wiki
  • Gas pipes (normal and insulated — insulation only affects heat transfer, not strength) burst at 60,795 kPa mechanical pressure differential. Liquid pipes burst at only 6,080 kPa — 10% of a gas pipe. This resolves the old "verify pipe tier vs. tank burst" open item: the Large Tank (60,000 kPa) is the actual weakest link on the gas side, slightly under the pipe's own 60,795 kPa, so RELIEF_HIGH at 45,000 kPa already has the right target. The liquid side is a separate, much lower-headroom concern — see Open Items.Stationeers Community Wiki / Steam Community discussion
  • Powered Vent has no internal pressure limiter — unlike the Active Vent, which self-caps via its own PressureInternal/PressureExternal logic types and won't over-pressurize the network it's filling. A Powered Vent left running against a closed pipe network will keep pumping past the pipe's burst point. This is what caused the pipe-bursting issue and is why vents are now IC-gated below instead of left always-on.Steam Community discussion
  • Condensation Valve: one-way liquid drain from a gas pipe network into a liquid pipe network; control mode is manual/passive, not an IC10-scriptable device. Purge Valve does the reverse (drains gas out of a liquid network) — used deliberately in Area 400 below to recover CO2 gas from the Liquid Tank, not just a related device to note.Stationeers Community Wiki + Steam Community discussion
  • The Temperature IC10 logic type reads/writes in Kelvin, confirmed against known IC10 behavior — this is what exposed the old script's dead CO2-drain branch above.Known IC10 behavior, cross-checked during this session
  • "Compressor" is not a real Stationeers device — confirmed by Ryan directly after searches kept failing to turn up a device by that name. The correct device for forcibly moving gas between two pipe networks against a large pressure differential is the Turbo Volume Pump (moves gas up to 10× faster than a standard Volume Pump, adjustable 0–100L valve, ~200W base + 6W/L). Volume Pump/Turbo Volume Pump don't stop moving gas on their own regardless of downstream pressure — same reason our IC10 hysteresis gating on tank pressure is required, not optional.Confirmed directly by Ryan; Turbo Volume Pump specs via Stationeers Community Wiki
  • Electrolyzer autoignition risk. If the electrolyzer's output network (where H2 and O2 share a pipe before being split off) rises above 300°C (573K), the mix autoignites — converts the O2/H2 to steam and causes further electrolyzer output to autocombust too. This is why the electrolyzer isn't left unscripted like the old O2 Supply description implied: atmospherics_electrolyzer.ic10 below trips a safety vent + shuts the unit off well before that threshold.Stationeers Community Wiki
  • Radiator type matters for the Area 200 cooling stage. Medium Convection Radiator exchanges heat with the surrounding atmosphere through a grill — it has "no use in a vacuum." Medium Radiator (radiative, non-convection) radiates heat directly into space and works with no atmosphere present. Area 200's radiators sit exterior, dumping heat into Mars' thin atmosphere to condense CO2 — that's the radiative device, not the Convection one, which would do close to nothing out there.Stationeers Community Wiki (Medium Radiator) + stationeering.substack.com, Life Support III: Radiative Cooling
  • Purge Valve recovers gas from a liquid tank on purpose — this is a documented, commonly-used pattern, not a workaround. Draining existing gas headspace from a liquid line drops local pressure, which pushes more of the liquid to evaporate to refill it; setting the Purge Valve's pressure limit toward 0 makes this aggressive. The known failure mode: evaporated gas can "leak through" the Purge Valve and re-condense on the gas side, so the standard fix is a Condensation Valve immediately after it (opposite direction from the main one) draining any re-condensed liquid back to the liquid line, to prevent pipe ruptures. This is what atmospherics_co2supply.ic10 below now does with Area 300's liquid CO2 instead of tapping raw Mars air a second time.Stationeers Community Wiki (Purge Valve) + Steam Community discussions ("How to pump out gas from a liquid tank?")
  • Insulated tank variants are real, separate structures — Large Tank (Insulated) and Large Liquid Tank (Insulated) are built from insulated tank kits (Kit (Tank Insulated) / ItemKitLiquidTankInsulated) rather than the standard kits, same 50,000 L capacity and ~60,000 kPa burst as the non-insulated version. Insulation only affects heat transfer with the surrounding environment, not pressure rating — useful here for keeping the Mars-air raw buffer's pressure from swinging with exterior temperature, and for keeping the liquid CO2 tanks from warming enough to re-vaporize their contents before the Purge Valve can draw them off.Stationeers Community Wiki (Tank) + Steam Community discussions
  • Ice Crusher processes Oxite and Nitrice into gas, not just liquid. It has two output ports — gas (top, yellow pipe) and liquid (bottom, blue pipe) — and its crush speed is controlled by a Setting parameter (Kelvin) writable via IC10, same as an On/Off logic type for gating. Oxite crushes into roughly 90% O2 / 10% N2 — not pure oxygen, has to be filtered same as everything else in this build.Stationeers Community Wiki (Ice Crusher, Ice (Oxite))
  • N2O's in-game triple point is around −21°C, confirmed directly from the Stationpedia phase-change graph (not just community sources) — well warmer than CO2's 218K/−55°C. That means N2O's liquid region opens up at far lower pressure/higher temperature than anything else piped in this build; a pressure that would keep CO2 comfortably gaseous will condense N2O. The graph's exact curve shape (how much pressure headroom exists at a given temperature above the triple point) wasn't fully read off the screenshot — treat as directional until confirmed against a live Pipe Analyzer reading once the branch is built.In-game Stationpedia phase graph, screenshot-confirmed by Ryan
  • Liquid Digital Valve is a real, separate device from the regular (gas-side) Digital Valve — confirmed directly by Ryan after this was flagged as an open question. Used for TK-301's relief valve since that tank sits on a liquid pipe network, not a gas one.Confirmed directly by Ryan
  • "Volatiles" was renamed to "Methane" in a game update; Hydrogen is a separate gas type entirely. Full in-game gas enum: Oxygen, Nitrogen, Carbon Dioxide, Volatiles/Methane, Pollutant, Water, Nitrous Oxide, plus liquid variants of each, and Hydrogen/Liquid Hydrogen (added separately — combusts into pure water when reacting with oxygen, used as rocket fuel). Confirms Methane and Hydrogen are two distinct gases, not two names for the same thing — matters here because atmospherics_recapture.ic10's old filtrationVolatiles only ever handled Methane; Hydrogen needed its own filter cartridge added to the same unit.StationeersLua enum docs (orbitalfoundrymodteam.github.io), Filtration wiki page
  • Volatile Ice melts into Methane + Hydrogen gas. Same ice-mining family as Oxite (→O2/N2) and Nitrice (→N2O) — each ice type breaks down into a specific gas pair when processed or melted. If Volatile Ice ends up inside the base (a real hazard: hauled in on a haulage run, dropped, carried through an airlock), it releases both gases directly into the room atmosphere — this is why Ryan wants Recapture to treat any Methane or Hydrogen reading the same way it already treats Pollutant, as something to scrub immediately rather than tolerate.Confirmed directly by Ryan; Ice (Oxite)/Ice Crusher wiki entries for the broader ice-type pattern

One source (a Substack "Thermal Conditioning & Gas Separation" guide) surfaced oddly-named devices ("TEC-V", "PGL filtration") that don't match any known vanilla Stationeers device — likely a heavily-modded build or a bad fetch/summary. Its specific numbers (293–303K window, 13.79 kPa/K ratio, 48,636 kPa max) were not used anywhere in this design; treat that source as unreliable.

Open Items

  • Resolved Human CO2 tolerance. Ryan has directly confirmed 20% CO2 is fine for humans in this game — overrides the wiki's >1%-causes-impairment claim. No longer treated as open; don't re-flag this or second-guess it against wiki figures going forward.

  • Resolved RELIEF_HIGH (45,000 kPa) — confirmed the Large Tank's 60,000 kPa burst rating is the actual limiting factor (gas pipes burst slightly higher, at 60,795 kPa), so 45,000 kPa (75% of tank burst) is the right target. No longer open.

  • Unresolved Liquid CO2 line is a much lower-headroom part of the system than anything else here. No relief valve or pressure monitoring exists on the liquid side yet.
